Executive summary

This project is building a new generation of smart payment terminals and merchant devices, so that more people and businesses can take part in the digital economy.

The objective is not simply another card machine. The broader vision is a platform that lets anyone sell, transact, and generate income from a connected payment and value-added-services terminal.

The device combines payment acceptance with airtime, data, electricity, and other digital products. That creates a path for merchants, informal traders, entrepreneurs, and people who do not have a traditional shop.

The long-term vision is a small business in a box: a physical device on a software platform, through which a person sells products and services and earns from each transaction.

Red Face is responsible for the software engineering, product development, system architecture, and the technical implementation already running in the prototype.

2

The core problem

Millions of people can sell, and still do not have:

  • Traditional retail infrastructure
  • An affordable point-of-sale system
  • Business premises
  • Payment infrastructure they can actually switch on
  • Digital sales tools
  • A second income that does not require a shop
Instead of asking people to build a business before they can use the technology, give them technology that helps them start earning.

A person at a street corner, a taxi rank, a school, an event, or inside a community could operate a terminal and sell airtime, mobile data, electricity, digital services, tickets, payment services, and other approved products. The terminal is an income tool, not only a card machine.

3

Product vision

A smart merchant terminal ecosystem has three parts.

Physical terminal

Card, contactless, and cash where it applies. Airtime, data, electricity, and other VAS. Receipts, history, configuration, and remote software updates.

Terminal software

A simple screen for sales, services, transaction history, service status, settings, and software updates. The platform controls what the device is allowed to do.

Cloud platform

Registration, pairing, merchants, configuration, releases, health, transactions, audit, revocation, VAS, and operational reporting.

4

What Red Face has already built

The working prototype shows that one platform can register, pair, watch, configure, update, and revoke a fleet of terminals, and can sell both payments and digital services.

CapabilityIn the prototype
Terminal registrationEach device gets a unique terminal identity.
Secure pairingA one-time pairing code is exchanged for a device credential. The code cannot be reused.
Heartbeat and healthThe platform sees connectivity, application health, backend reachability, and battery.
Remote configurationCash, card, tap, QR, receipts, and auto-update can be switched from the operations desk.
Software releasesA release is published to a terminal. The device does not install it merely because it exists.
Update verificationThe package hash must match the published release before install.
Installation lifecycleDetected, download, verify, install, health check, completed. Each heartbeat advances one step.
RollbackA failed verification or a failed health check returns the terminal to the previous version.
RevocationA remote revoke invalidates the paired device credential.
PaymentsCash completes for real in the ledger. Card, tap, and QR run as a practice flow until a certified reader and an acquirer are connected. The product does not ask anyone to type a card number or a PIN.
Transaction historySales are recorded with clear outcomes: success, pending, failed, timeout, duplicate, and provider error.
VASAirtime, data, electricity, vouchers, and SMS are already wired to a live South African provider, SIMcloud. Data is grouped the way the networks name the bundles.
OperationsMerchant desk, terminals, reconciliation, and a settlement view.
The lifecycle is enforced, not described: update detected → download → verify → install → health check → completed. A bad package or a failed health check ends in rollback.

5

The VAS and airtime opportunity

Value-added services are a commercial opportunity beside card payments. The same terminal can sell digital products.

  • Mobile: airtime, data bundles, and related mobile services.
  • Utilities: electricity and other prepaid utilities.
  • Digital products: tickets, vouchers, and other approved products.

The platform is meant to own the commercial and technical layer through which these services are sold. A third-party provider is already connected so the product can enter the market without waiting for direct network contracts. The first provider is SIMcloud. Catalogue, pricing bands, and order states are live in the prototype.

The longer strategy is direct or wholesale relationships with service providers and mobile networks, where that is commercially and legally sound. That is how the company gains more control of pricing, margin, availability, routing, settlement, and the customer experience.

Today the live provider sell price equals the provider cost, so the demonstrated margin is zero until a markup is set. The practice catalogue uses a sample margin so the commercial shape is visible. Customer price, provider cost, and margin stay separate in the books.

The bigger commercial vision

This is not a single card machine. It is a distributed sales network. A person does not need a traditional shop to take part.

A young person could operate a terminal in their community and earn a margin on airtime, data, electricity, or other approved services.

The same system can sit at a taxi rank, a school, an event, a market, a salon, a restaurant, a small shop, a community centre, or a workplace. That is a network of micro-merchants. The terminal is a tool for commerce, payments, and income.

Future product concepts

The same hardware and software can carry specialised jobs beyond a shop counter. One concept already discussed is ticketing and access: ticket sales, ticket checks, airtime at the event, entry, transport-related services, and digital vouchers. Several businesses, one device platform.

9

Development strategy

Build on the prototype. Do not restart it.

Phase 1 — Foundation, already demonstrated

Registration, pairing, heartbeat, configuration, releases, package verification, installation, health checks, rollback, revocation, cash sales, practice card and tap, transaction history, the operations desk, and a live VAS connection for airtime, data, electricity, vouchers, and SMS.

Phase 2 — Commercial connections still required

Certified payment hardware. An acquirer and payment provider, so a real card can be taken. A markup and commission engine on VAS. Durable merchant accounts. Reconciliation against the acquirer, not only the sandbox ledger.

Phase 3 — Commercial platform

Merchant dashboard, controlled catalogue and pricing, commission management, provider routing, settlement, reporting, and customer management.

Phase 4 — Scale

More terminals, more merchants, more VAS providers, wholesale relationships where they make sense, broader payment methods, and further applications on the same device.
